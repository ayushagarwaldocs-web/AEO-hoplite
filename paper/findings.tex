\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Potential access obstacles are common, mostly not expressed in agent-specific rules, and restrictions
  target training.} Of the organisational sites, \BarAny\% showed at least one potential obstacle on a first-party
  crawling route (\BarNoEdge\% without research-client edge refusals), but only \BarNamed\% disallowed an AI search
  crawler by name; for \BarImplicitOnly\%, none of the flagged conditions (a wildcard rule, an unreachable robots.txt,
  an edge refusal or JavaScript-dependent text) was expressed through an agent-specific rule. These are measured
  conditions, not demonstrated exclusion from answers. Sites restricted training crawlers far more often than answer
  agents: \PairGptFirst{} block OpenAI's training crawler but not its search crawler, and \PairGptSecondWord{} the
  reverse, an asymmetry the Tranco sample shares.
  \item \textbf{Endorsement had the largest effect when pages were supplied.} With the pages in the model's context,
  a third party's recommendation raised the chance that a brand was named by \EMenAllThirdPartyU{} points pooled over
  12 hosted API models, significantly in every one, and by \FlEMenAllThirdPartyU{} points across \FlNModelsTwo{}
  additional API models from the developers of assistants such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, Grok and Claude. The best
  edit to the brand's own page, a citation package, added \HTwoBestEffectU{} points. The recommendation adds
  information as well as a third-party source, so the experiment does not separate the two.
  \item \textbf{Evidence packages helped and were repeated, but emphasis helped too.} In the main analysis, citation,
  statistics and quotation packages beat both the unedited page and a boilerplate placebo, and answers that named the brand repeated
  the added sources and figures far more often than persuasive phrases (by a keyword detector). Keyword stuffing helped
  about as much as statistics, while restating the page as questions and answers, a summary or a table had no
  significant effect.
  \item \textbf{On longer synthetic pages, matching the question rivalled endorsement.} When every page was about six
  times longer, evidence effects shrank by about half, superlatives stopped working, and repeating the question's
  words rose from \RLongMainKeywordStuffing{} to \RLongAltKeywordStuffing{} points, about as much as endorsement.
  \item \textbf{When models searched for themselves, being found came first.} When \AgNModelsW{} API models searched a
  synthetic corpus of 390 pages, the brand was named in \AgBaseMen\% of baseline answers instead of \AgBaseMenCtx\%
  with the pages supplied, a contrast that also changes the candidate pool and tools. Citation, statistics and
  instruction packages had no detectable effect, and the target page was opened in only \AgBaseOpen\% of baseline
  answers, while keyword stuffing and an answer-first summary put the page among the search results
  \AgESurfKeywordStuffingU{} and \AgESurfAnswerFirstU{} points more often. Endorsement still raised mentions, by
  \AgEMenThirdPartyU{} points, and was the only intervention that raised the chance of being named first.
  \item \textbf{Most models followed an instruction embedded in the page; Anthropic's did not.} A one-sentence
  instruction in the page text raised mentions by \EMenAllInjectionU{} points, significantly in \NSigMenInjectionW{} of
  the 12 models, and answers almost never disclosed it. Claude models moved against it or, in Claude Sonnet 5.5's
  case, showed no significant increase and called the instruction out in \RCallSonnetfivePct\% of answers. In the
  search task it had no detectable effect.
  \item \textbf{Page position was not a reliable lever.} The pre-specified position effect was not supported once the
  test respected the page order that all models shared within a trial ($p = \HFiveP$); an earlier test that ignored
  this sharing had overstated it (\cref{app:prereg}).
  \item \textbf{The most used assistant showed no retrieval in our audit.} In a snapshot audit, ChatGPT's free tier
  answered all \AudCNQW{} product questions with no visible web search or cited source, while Perplexity searched every
  time and drew \AudPThirdPct\% of its citations from third parties. Where an assistant does not retrieve, page edits
  and crawler access can matter only through what earlier crawls put into the model; the interface cannot show which
  route an answer took.
\end{itemize}
