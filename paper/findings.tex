\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Potential access obstacles are common and mostly implicit.} Of the organisational sites, \BarAny\% showed
  at least one potential obstacle on a first-party crawling route (\BarNoEdge\% without research-client edge refusals),
  but only \BarNamed\% disallowed an AI search crawler by name; for \BarImplicitOnly\%, none of the flagged conditions
  was expressed through an agent-specific rule. Sites restricted training crawlers far more often than answer agents.
  These are measured conditions, not demonstrated exclusion from answers.
  \item \textbf{With pages supplied, endorsement and evidence packages raised mentions.} A third party's recommendation raised the chance that a brand was named by \EMenAllThirdPartyU{}
  points across 12 hosted API models, significantly in every one and more than any edit to the brand's own page; the
  strongest single edit, a citation package, added \HTwoBestEffectU{} points. Citation, statistics and quotation packages beat
  both the unedited page and a boilerplate placebo of similar length, and keyword stuffing helped about as much as
  statistics. A follow-up on the brand's own page found that recommending rather than neutral wording added \AttrRecU{}
  points, and attributing the recommendation to an outside reviewer \AttrExtU{} more; on a separate guide page, the
  reviewer's neutral sentence named the brand \SiteNeuU{} points more often than on the brand's own page. An instruction
  embedded in page
  text raised mentions in most models but not in Anthropic's.
  \item \textbf{When models searched for themselves, the ranking of interventions changed.} With \AgNModelsW{} API
  models searching a 390-page synthetic corpus, the brand was named in \AgBaseMen\% of baseline answers instead of
  \AgBaseMenCtx\% with the pages supplied. Citation, statistics and instruction packages had no detectable effect there,
  text matching the question made the page easier to find, and endorsement still raised mentions. These are contrasts
  between task regimes, not evidence that retrieval mediates the effects.
\end{itemize}
