# 6. Earned media and off-site presence: the biggest lever

Answer engines triangulate. When deciding what to say about you, they lean on what *others*
say: reviews, news, comparisons, forums, video and reference sites. In controlled comparisons
across verticals, AI search showed "a systematic and overwhelming bias towards earned media
(third-party, authoritative sources) over brand-owned and social content", far more than
Google's classic results did ([Chen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08919)).

## 6.1 Where answers come from in 2026

Large citation studies agree on the broad picture, if not the exact numbers:

- **Reddit, YouTube, Wikipedia and LinkedIn** dominate cited domains overall
  ([Peec AI, 30M citations, March 2026](https://docs.peec.ai/research/most-cited-domains-in-ai-search)).
- Engines differ. Reddit is especially prominent in Perplexity and Google's AI features,
  Wikipedia in ChatGPT, YouTube in AI Overviews. Review platforms (G2, Yelp, Trustpilot) and
  editorial "best of" lists matter for commercial queries.
- The long tail is huge: most citations go to domains outside any top-100 list, so niche
  trade publications and specialist communities matter in your category.

## 6.2 Find your citation gaps

1. Run your prompt set (chapter 7) across engines and log every cited URL.
2. Group the citations by domain and type: review site, publisher, community, video,
   competitor-owned, your own site.
3. For each high-frequency source where you are absent or misrepresented, decide on a play:

| Source type | Legitimate play |
|---|---|
| Review platforms | Ask real customers for reviews; respond to reviews; keep profiles complete and current |
| Editorial comparisons and "best X" lists | Brief the writers with accurate data; offer trials and access; correct factual errors politely |
| Trade press and news | Pitch original data and genuine news; make spokespeople available |
| Reddit and forums | Have real staff participate openly, answer questions, disclose affiliation, follow each community's rules |
| YouTube and podcasts | Partner with creators (with disclosure); publish your own explainers with transcripts |
| Wikipedia and Wikidata | See [chapter 5](05-entity-and-structured-data.md): only factual, sourced, conflict-of-interest-compliant edits |

## 6.3 Reviews

- Reviews feed both training data and live answers about quality, support and value.
- Ask every customer, not just happy ones; review gating violates several platforms' rules.
- In the US, the FTC's rule on fake reviews (effective October 2024) prohibits fake or
  AI-generated reviews, buying positive or negative reviews, and undisclosed insider reviews.
  Similar rules exist elsewhere.

## 6.4 Communities

Community content is cited heavily because it reads as unbiased. That only lasts while it
*is* unbiased. Astroturfing gets accounts banned and threads removed, and when it is exposed
it becomes the story an answer engine retells about you. Participate as yourself, add real
expertise, and let customers speak for you.

## 6.5 Digital PR built on data

The most reliable route into earned media is giving journalists something new: original
research (chapter 4.4), expert commentary, transparent incident write-ups, and genuinely
useful tools. Measure success by **AI-cited coverage**, not just links: did the pieces you
earned start appearing as citations in your prompt set?

## 6.6 Small and niche players

Engines show a "big brand bias": well-known names are recommended by default. Niche players
can counter it by:

- Owning narrow, specific intents ("for agencies under 20 people", "for Rust developers")
  where big brands are weakly relevant
- Earning specialist coverage (trade newsletters, niche communities, expert blogs)
- Publishing the definitive data or guide in their niche
