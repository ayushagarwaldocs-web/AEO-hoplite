Title:
Crawler Access and Content Effects in Answer Engine Optimization: A Website Audit and Controlled Experiments

Authors:
Ayush Agarwal

Abstract (1592 characters; arXiv allows 1,920):
Answer engines such as ChatGPT and Perplexity name organisations and cite sources. We study whether crawlers can reach a site's own pages and how page content affects selection. Study 1 audits 569 organisational websites in 22 sectors and 703 popular domains. Of the organisational sites, 35.0% showed at least one potential obstacle on a first-party crawling route (19.0% without research-client refusals at the network edge), mostly not expressed in agent-specific robots.txt rules, and sites restricted AI training crawlers far more often than answer agents; these are measured obstacles, not demonstrated exclusion. Study 2 is a pre-specified experiment with fictional brands: 14 page interventions in 12 hosted API models, replicated on 11 more. With pages supplied, a third party's recommendation raised the chance that a brand was named by 37.4 percentage points, more than any edit to the brand's own page; citation and statistics packages added 21.1 and 13.7 points, keyword stuffing also helped, and an instruction embedded in the page worked on most models but not on Anthropic's. In a follow-up, recommending rather than neutral wording on the brand's page added 10.1 points, and crediting an outside reviewer 3.1 more. When four models searched a synthetic corpus themselves, the brand was named in 22% of baseline answers instead of 53%, evidence and instruction effects were no longer detectable, and text matching the question made pages easier to find. Outcomes record whether answers name the brand, not whether they recommend it. We release the toolkit, benchmark and data.
