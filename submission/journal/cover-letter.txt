Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Crawler Access and Content Effects in Answer Engine Optimization: A Website Audit and Controlled Experiments", for publication as a research article in Information Processing & Management.

Answer engines such as ChatGPT and Perplexity answer questions by naming organisations and citing sources, and organisations now try to optimise for them. The paper asks how often organisations' own websites present obstacles to the crawlers that answer engines use, and which page interventions change whether a brand is named. Study 1 audits 569 organisational websites in 22 sectors and 703 popular domains from four Tranco rank bands: 35.0% of the organisational sites showed at least one potential obstacle on a first-party crawling route, mostly not stated in agent-specific robots.txt rules, and sites restrict AI training crawlers far more often than answer agents. Study 2 is a pre-specified experiment with fictional brands in 12 hosted API models, with a follow-up on 11 more. With the pages supplied, a third party's recommendation raised the chance that a brand was named by 37.4 percentage points, more than any edit to the brand's own page; when models searched a corpus themselves, the effects of evidence edits were no longer detectable and text matching the question made pages easier to find.

The paper is explicit about its scope: the audit measures potential obstacles rather than exclusion from answers, and the experiments use synthetic pages and API models rather than deployed assistants. I believe it fits the journal's interest in information retrieval, information access and the evaluation of systems built on large language models. The code, benchmark, model responses and derived data are released with the paper.
