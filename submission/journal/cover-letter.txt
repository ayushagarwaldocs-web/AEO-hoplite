Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "The Discoverability Gap: Access Barriers and Content Effects in Answer
Engine Optimization", for publication as a research article in Information Processing & Management.

Answer engines such as ChatGPT and Perplexity now answer many questions by naming a few organisations, and
organisations try to influence which ones, a practice called answer engine (or generative engine) optimisation.
Existing work studies either whether AI crawlers may fetch sites or how page edits change answers. The manuscript
measures both and compares them:

- Study 1 measures access barriers to AI answer engines on 569 organisational websites in 22 sectors and 703 randomly
  sampled popular sites: robots.txt rules for 24 agent tokens, refusals at the network edge and text that only
  JavaScript produces. One organisational site in three has at least one barrier, mostly unintended.
- Study 2 is a pre-specified experiment with fictional brands: 14 page treatments in 12 hosted language models,
  replicated on 10 models behind widely used assistants, with checks on long pages, sampling and the outcome measure.
  An agentic version, in which four models search a 390-page pool themselves, shows that edits which change only
  how a page is read lose their effect when the page must first be found. A live audit of Perplexity, Gemini and
  ChatGPT shows how production assistants retrieve and cite.

I believe the work fits the journal's interest in information retrieval, information access and the evaluation of
systems built on large language models. The code, benchmark, all model responses and derived data are released with
the paper.
