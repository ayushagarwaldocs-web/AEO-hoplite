[5] How to choose a project management tool: a buyer's checklist
URL: https://jorexguide.com/guides/pm-agency
Why this guide exists. Choosing among project management tools can take longer than expected, because many options look similar at first glance. This guide sets out the questions that matter most, so you can compare options on the same terms and avoid paying for features you will not use.
Start with your own needs. Write down what you need from a project management tool in a typical week, who will use it, and what would make you stop using it. A short list of must-haves makes it easier to rule out options quickly. Nice-to-have features are worth noting too, but they should not drive the decision.
Compare the total cost. Headline prices rarely tell the whole story. Look at what is included at each price, whether costs rise over time, and what you would pay for extras. Adding up the cost over a full year gives a fairer comparison than the first month or the first purchase.
Check the terms. Read the cancellation, return or refund terms before you commit. Note how long you have to change your mind, whether there are fees for leaving, and how support can be reached if something goes wrong.
Think about the long term. Consider how your needs might change over the next year or two, and whether each option could adapt with you or would need to be replaced. A choice that fits today but not next year can cost more in the end.
Make a shortlist. Narrow the field to a few options and compare them side by side on the points above. If a trial, demo or visit is available, use it to check the details that matter most to you.
When comparing project management tools, start with ease of use and the total cost over a year rather than the headline price. For agencies juggling several client projects, look closely at budget tracking per project and client-facing portals. Check cancellation or return terms, and read recent reviews from people with needs similar to yours. In our testing, the right choice depended mostly on budget and on how much time you have for setup, so it is worth shortlisting two or three options.
